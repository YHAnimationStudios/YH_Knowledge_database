## Assistant (sent 2026-11-03T08:34:15.355Z)

    I don't yet know why the glass goes white under ray traced translucency. I checked the material and camera settings, but I haven't changed or rendered anything, so the causes below are likely rather than confirmed.
    
    **What I found**
    
    - **Sequence and glass:** the open sequence is `0253A_E05_S05_C1`. The canopy is the `MI_Me_262_Glass` slot on `SK_Me_262`, with no per-actor overrides. It's an instance of `M_Glass_Dirty_01`, which is a child of `M_Glass_DirtyMaster` from the Advanced Glass Pack.
    - **Material settings:**
      - It uses Translucent blend and DefaultLit shading.
      - Lighting mode is Surface Per Pixel Lighting.
      - Refraction method is Index of Refraction.
      - Two Sided is forced on in the instance.
      - Opacity is 0.25 and Refraction is 1.0.
      - Fresnel nodes feed both the Opacity and Refraction inputs.
      - SSR on translucency is on, and the material is not a thin surface.
    - **Camera:** the shot camera already has Translucency Type set to Ray Traced, with ray traced refraction on, max roughness 0.6, and 8 primary and 8 secondary hit events.
    
    **Likely causes**
    
    1. **Stacked layers.** The canopy is a closed, two-sided shell with frames and an inner pane. The ray tracer treats every surface crossing as a hit event, so each crossing adds the 0.25 opacity and the Fresnel reflection on top. Raster draws one blended layer, which would explain why the pilot washes out only in ray tracing.
    2. **Refraction behaves differently.** With Index of Refraction and ray traced refraction on, the Fresnel-driven Refraction value is treated as a real IOR at each crossing. The result is bent, bright sky and cloud reflection instead of the pilot. Raster only does a cheap screen-space offset.
    3. **Fresnel everywhere.** Fresnel pushes opacity up at glancing angles, and the camera looks along the canopy at a glancing angle. Together with roughness 0.06 and the sky reflection, that gives the milky look.
    
    **What I'd try, in order, on a duplicate of `MI_Me_262_Glass` so the shared master isn't touched**
    
    1. Set Refraction to a constant 1.0. If Fresnel still modulates it, override it in the instance or in a copy of the master. This tells us whether refraction is the culprit.
    2. Lower Opacity to about 0.05–0.1 and Fresnel_Falloff to about 2–3, since the ray tracer accumulates layers.
    3. If it still looks milky, make a thin-glass variant of the master: Thin Surface on, Refraction method None, or the ThinTranslucent shading model, with no Fresnel-driven opacity.
    4. On the camera, lower Max Primary Hit Events from 8 to 2–4 and turn off ray traced refraction. That's a quick check of whether stacked layers are the problem.
    
    Whichever settings work, I'd apply them to this one material instance rather than to the whole glass pack.
